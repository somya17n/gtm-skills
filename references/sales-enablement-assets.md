# Sales Enablement Asset Templates

Reference for the `sales-enablement` skill: the sales deck framework, case study brief format, and buyer persona card template. One-pager, ROI calculator, and proposal structures live in the main SKILL.md — this file covers the two asset types that need more room.

---

## Sales Deck — 10-12 Slide Framework

1. **Current-world problem** — the pain the buyer lives with today
2. **Cost of the problem** — what inaction costs, in time/money/risk
3. **The shift happening** — market or technology change creating urgency
4. **Your approach** — how you solve it differently
5. **Product walkthrough** — 3-4 key workflows, not a full feature tour
6. **Proof points** — metrics, logos, third-party recognition
7. **Case study** — one customer story told well, not a logo wall
8. **Implementation/timeline** — how they get from signed to live
9. **ROI/value** — expected return and payback period
10. **Pricing overview** — transparent, tiered if applicable
11. **Next steps/CTA** — a clear action with a timeline

### Principles

- Story arc, not feature tour: the world has a problem → there's a better way → here's proof → here's how to get there
- One idea per slide — if a slide needs two points, split it into two slides
- Design for presenting, not reading — slides support the conversation, they don't replace it

### Customization by buyer type

| Buyer | Emphasize | De-emphasize |
|---|---|---|
| Technical buyer | Architecture, security, integrations, API | ROI math, business metrics |
| Economic buyer | ROI, payback period, total cost, risk | Technical implementation detail |
| Champion | Internal selling points, quick wins, peer proof | Deep technical/financial detail |

## Case Study Briefs (Sales Format)

Sales case studies differ from marketing case studies: marketing tells a story, sales needs fast-access proof mid-call. Keep it short, outcome-focused, and tagged for retrieval.

**Structure:**
1. Customer profile — industry, company size, buyer role
2. Challenge — 2-3 sentences on what they struggled with
3. Solution — 1-2 sentences on what they implemented
4. Results — 3 specific before/after metrics
5. Pull quote — one sentence from the customer, verbatim if available
6. Tags — industry, use case, company size, persona (so a rep can pull "show me a healthcare example" instantly)

## Buyer Persona Card

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Role/title | Common titles and reporting structure |
| Goals | What success looks like for them specifically |
| Pains | What frustrates them day to day |
| Top objections | The 3-5 objections this role raises most (cross-reference `objection-pre-handler` output) |
| Evaluation criteria | How they judge solutions |
| Buying process role | Decision-maker, influencer, blocker, or champion |
| Messaging angle | The single sentence that resonates most with this persona |

### Persona archetypes

- **Economic buyer** — signs the check, cares about ROI and risk
- **Technical buyer** — evaluates the product, cares about capability and integration
- **End user** — uses it daily, cares about ease and workflow fit
- **Champion** — advocates internally, needs ammunition to sell on your behalf
- **Blocker** — opposes the purchase; understand the concern to neutralize it, don't ignore it

## Common Mistakes Across Asset Types

- Proposals and decks that lead with features instead of the buyer's own stated pain
- ROI calculators with the formula hidden — a rep can't defend math they can't see
- One-pagers that try to cover everything instead of the 3 differentiators that actually matter for this buyer
- Playbooks nobody owns — assign an owner and review quarterly, or it goes stale within two quarters
